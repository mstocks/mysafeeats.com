The difference

Labels lead with claims. MySafeEats leads with ingredients.

The front of a package is designed to sell. Phrases like “wheat free,” “made with whole grains,” or “all natural” can sound reassuring while telling you very little about gluten. The ingredient list is where the facts are, so that’s where MySafeEats looks first.

FAQ

Good questions, straight answers.

Still wondering about something? Get in touch.

Do I need to download an app?

No. MySafeEats is a web app that runs in your phone’s browser, on iPhone or Android. Open it, allow camera access when your browser asks so it can read barcodes, and start scanning. If you like, add it to your home screen for one-tap access.

Does MySafeEats replace reading the label?

No. MySafeEats is a shopping aid that helps you screen products quickly. Ingredient data can be incomplete or out of date, and recipes change, so always check the package before you eat something.

Where does the ingredient information come from?

When you scan a UPC barcode, MySafeEats looks up the product in a global food database and retrieves the ingredient list on record for that product. That list is then checked against your ingredient list.

Which ingredients does it look for?

By default, MySafeEats checks for wheat, rye, triticale, barley, malt, regular oats (oats not labeled gluten-free), brewer’s yeast, and yeast extract. Dextrin and food starch are flagged for you to verify, especially in meats, because they can be made from wheat. You can switch any item off and add other ingredients you want to avoid.

Can I use it for allergens other than gluten?

Yes. Add any ingredient name to your list and every product you scan will be checked against it. That works whether you have celiac disease plus other sensitivities, or an allergen that standard lists don’t cover.

Does it account for cross-contact or shared equipment?

MySafeEats checks the ingredients listed for a product. Cross-contact risks, such as shared equipment or facilities, aren’t always part of an ingredient list. Check the package for advisory statements, and contact the manufacturer if you’re unsure.

What if a product isn’t found or has no ingredient list?

MySafeEats can only check ingredients it can retrieve. If a product isn’t in the database or its ingredient list is missing, read the package label directly.

If a product is “safe for your list,” is it guaranteed gluten-free?

No. “Safe for your list” means nothing on your list was found in the ingredient data available. It isn’t a guarantee or a certification. When in doubt, check the label and look for a certified gluten-free mark.
